The glass plate contains 3 grayscale exposures in BGR order. I split it into 3 equal parts, keep B fixed, and shift G and R over a search window from −15 to 15 pixels.

For each shift, I compare the center 40% of the images so the borders won't decide the match.

I use NCC: both crops are mean-centered and normalized, then their dot product is calculated. The shift with the highest NCC wins.

An exhaustive search at full resolution is too slow for the large TIFF scans. I use an image pyramid by repeatedly shrinking both channels to half size until the width is at most 400 pixels.

After finding a displacement, at the next larger level, I double that estimate and search only a small radius around it.

Repeating this process gives a fast full-resolution alignment with the same NCC score.

Raw NCC works when matching objects with similar brightness. BUT Emir's blue robe is bright in one channel and dark in another. The same region has very different values, so raw NCC goes wrong.

I use Sobel to detect brightness changes, then combine them as sqrt(Gx²+Gy²). NCC compares where boundaries occur instead of brightness itself, so the objects align better.
