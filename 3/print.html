exploring pretrained flow models

forward process | Euler sampling | classifier-free guidance | image editing | flow-based optical illusions

1.1 · constructing noisy training inputs

I reused one Gaussian noise tensor and interpolated it with the same clean image. As t increases, the clean-image contribution becomes stronger and the original structure becomes easier to recover.

xt = (1 − t)ε + tx1

1.2 · classical denoising

Gaussian blur reduces pixel-level noise, but the tradeoff becomes severe when the input contains little signal. At lower t, smoothing cannot reconstruct details that are overwhelmed by noise.

1.3 · one-step denoising

A single velocity prediction gives an endpoint estimate. It restores global structure more effectively than Gaussian blur, but errors remain because the velocity field changes along the path.

x̂1 = xt + (1 − t)vθ(xt, t, c)

1.4 · visualizing velocity

The predicted and ground-truth updates agree most clearly on large facial structures. The RMS heatmap exposes localized errors where the model must infer fine texture or ambiguous boundaries.

1.5 · iterative euler denoising

Euler sampling recomputes the velocity after every step, allowing the trajectory to bend as the image becomes cleaner. I used h = 0.02 from t = 0.50 to 1.00.

xt+h = xt + h vθ(xt, t, c)

Starting Euler integration from pure Gaussian noise produces diverse images under the generic prompt “a high quality photo.” The subject matter varies, while the samples share a polished photographic prior.

2.1 · classifier-free guidance

CFG pushes the conditional prediction away from the unconditional one. With guidance weight 7, the samples become more visually decisive, at the cost of some diversity.

vcfg = vc + w(vc − vu)

part b

flow matching from scratch

one-step MNIST denoiser | single-step regression | time-conditioned velocity model | class conditioning | classifier-free guidance.

1.2 · training a single-step denoiser

The forward corruption adds Gaussian noise with increasing standard deviation. I trained the unconditional UNet at σ = 0.5 using an L2 reconstruction objective.

z = x + σε L = E‖Dθ(z) − x‖²

1.2.2 · out-of-distribution noise

The denoiser is strongest near its training level σ = 0.5. Clean or lightly corrupted inputs can be over-smoothed, while very noisy inputs contain too little digit structure for a single prediction to recover reliably.

1.2.3 · denoising pure noise

With MSE and no conditioning, the model cannot choose a particular digit from independent noise. It is pushed toward a conditional average of the training distribution, producing soft centroid-like patterns rather than distinct samples.

2.2 · time-conditioned flow matching

Rather than predicting the clean digit directly, the UNet predicts velocity along a linear path from noise x0 to data x1. A scalar time embedding tells the network which part of the path it is evaluating.

xt = (1 − t)x0 + tx1 u = x1 − x0

2.3 · sampling the time-conditioned unet

Iterative sampling gradually turns noise into recognizable digit-like strokes. Later checkpoints improve coherence, although an unconditional model still mixes digit identities and produces ambiguous samples.

2.5 · class-conditioned flow matching

I added one-hot digit conditioning with 10% conditional dropout. During sampling, classifier-free guidance strengthens the requested class and reduces ambiguity between digit identities.

2.6 · class-conditioned sampling

Each column targets one digit and each row uses a different noise sample.

without the learning-rate scheduler

I removed the exponential scheduler and trained a fresh model with Adam at a fixed learning rate of 0.001, instead of starting at 0.01. The smaller constant rate avoids large updates without learning-rate decay. I kept the hidden dimension at 64, batch size at 64, and trained for 10 epochs.

The loss drops quickly at first and then improves more gradually. By epoch 10, the digits are recognizable across all ten classes and look broadly comparable to the scheduled results. This is a qualitative comparison of the saved samples.
